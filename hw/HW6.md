### A2

This just stuck with me as a sobering moment, almost. I read the whole paper, fascinated by the fact that they were proposing something taken so for granted today. We hammer home in class plenty that each layer of the network stack is completely focused on its job/scope and has no interest in the job/scope of the layers sandwiching it. It feels like an obvious way to design a system--a complete separation of concerns. BUT, its strange reading something like this because its essentially making an argument for a layering of the network stack, but it was 1984, and this was probably all pretty novel territory. Like we've brought up in class as well: the early networks were mostly small and between countable individuals. These people writing were looking ahead to design something better, almost anticipating the monstrosity that is the Internet today. And if people hadn't had these thoughts or ideas back then, the Internet likely couldn't exist in its current form today. Although plenty of bandaiding occurs still to things people in 1984 didn't anticipate, we're pretty lucky that system designers started having these conversations so early--and that's something that's very easy to take for granted.
